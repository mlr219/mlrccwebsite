Cases

What changes, in practice.

Four situations you might recognize. None of them needed more budget. Each one started with what the company already had.

A start-up built to scale

Our work

Situation
A founder with fifteen years in life-science commercialization, a strong network, and several product ideas, each promising on its own.
Complication
Every idea pulled in a different direction and competed for the same founder's time. Investors, partners and first clients were each hearing a different business.
What we did
We mapped the ideas onto one value chain to show how the products connect, which one starts the ball rolling, and how what is learned from each one feeds the next.
What changed
One business, one first offer, one target client and one story for investors, before any launch spend.

An established founder-led brand

Our work

Situation
A loyal customer base, a passionate founder, and an appetite for much more growth.
Complication
The plan was to grow online five times over. That would have been a good result, but the website was only 5% of sales, so even a fivefold jump wouldn't move the needle for the business as a whole. Meanwhile the founder was still running every part of it himself.
What we did
We mapped the different customer groups and what each could be worth. The largest opportunity sat with project and trade buyers, far bigger than anything the website could add. Along the way, the founder's deeper ambition came out: not a bigger shop, but an atelier with a brand of its own.
What changed
A growth plan built around those customer groups, with the website reworked to support them. Day-to-day operations moved to a partner at the same cost, freeing the founder to grow the brand. Each step is paid for by the growth of the one before.

A global luxury brand

From our founder's work

Situation
A heritage brand with strong client books, a great assortment and an aging client base.
Complication
Launches were designed for a young, fashion-led crowd, but long-standing clients were the ones buying them. The younger customers the brand did have almost never came back.
What we did
Among several moves to hedge an aging client base, we turned to the customer the brand had overlooked: younger clients with traditional taste, the ones who could give it longevity. We used what the brand already had, its client books, assortment and budget, and redesigned messaging and clienteling around their needs.
What changed
Retention among those younger customers rose 21% in the first year. Direct marketing sales grew 24%, and like-for-like sales in the pilot stores grew 17% while the rest stayed flat. All with no added budget.

A beauty brand

From our founder's work

Situation
Fast growth, a large store network and a steady stream of new products.
Complication
The company was built to win young, trend-led customers: most activity went into new collections and most of the ad budget went to teenagers, at a high cost per new customer. Yet most sales came from basics, bought by middle-aged women who kept slipping away.
What we did
We started with what the brand already had: the basics, the stores and its highest-potential customers. Every team focused on one priority group, mapped how they shop, and worked from a single shared calendar.
What changed
Within six months, return on ad spend doubled on targeted digital campaigns. Collection development was scaled back, which cut development costs, and the whole company worked to one commercial roadmap.
