We call them imaginary customers.

The customer a company is chasing but can't seem to win. It comes in three forms:

  • The one who isn't there

    Or there are too few of them to build a business on.

  • The one you don't have

    The customer the plan is written for, while someone else is actually buying.

  • The one you lose

    They come once, then the way the company runs lets them slip away.

How it shows up from the inside

  • Every team is busy, but results aren't moving.
  • The same decisions keep coming back to the table.
  • No two teams seem to be on the same page.
  • Holding last year's number takes more budget every year.

It looks like growth that stays out of reach, no matter how much effort and money go in.

Customer centricity is half the story.

Focus only on the customer and it fails, like any relationship where one side does all the work. A brand people buy because they want it, not because they need it, has to hold three things in balance: the brand, the customers and the organization, tied together by one promise.

When they don't line up, the cracks show in three places, often all at once.

Empty vessels
The brand stops at a product, a symbol or a set of values, so each team fills the gaps on its own.
Imaginary customers
The business serves a customer who isn't there, doesn't drive value, or gets pieced together from channel targets until no one recognizes them.
The emperor's new clothes
The organization believes it keeps the promise, but its targets, budgets and incentives work against it, and no one says so.
