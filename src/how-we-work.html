How we work

Growth holds when its foundation does.

Early growth often comes from a niche that understands you without being told. The next level needs a promise clear enough to win the people who won't work it out on their own, and a company built to keep it.

The Foundational Trifecta

Customer centricity is half the story. Focus only on the customer and it fails, like any relationship where one side does all the work. A desire-led brand has to hold three things in balance.

Brand
A brand with depth and a clear meaning for your customers, your teams and your partners. Not just a product, a symbol or a set of values.
Customers
The right ones, in order. Who's in, who's out, and who comes first. Not simply everyone you can sell to.
Organization
A business built to deliver the promise, from product to marketing to distribution, with targets, budgets and incentives that pull the same way.
The promise
What ties the brand to those customers and sets you apart from the competition.

Where the cracks show

When the three don't line up, the cracks show in three places, often all at once.

Empty vessels

The brand stops at a product, a symbol or a set of values, so each team fills the gaps on its own.

Imaginary customers

The business serves a customer who isn't there, doesn't drive value, or gets pieced together from channel targets until no one recognizes them.

The emperor's new clothes

The organization believes it keeps the promise, but its targets, budgets and incentives work against it, and no one says so.

The C(EO)X method

Every engagement follows the same order, sized to your stage. We set the direction and the order of work, help you build what comes first, and step back. You run it.

  1. Orient

    Where you are, and where you want to go

    We start with what's in your head, because the most important things are often so obvious to you that they never got written down: your product, how you imagine your brand, who you picture as your customer and who actually buys, and what you're building toward. Then we look outside, at the market, its growth and competition, and fuller profiles of your customers. That's where the first provocations come from.

  2. Architect

    What must be true for the promise to hold

    The provocations turn into two portraits, the brand and the customer, written as if they were two people. The promise is the common ground between them. It anchors the constitution, the North Star that stays fixed while things like style change with the times. From there, each function gets clear guidelines, the brand book among them.

  3. Chart

    What you're committing to

    With your priority customers set, we work backwards from where you want to be: over what horizon, what the plan is, and what it means for each area of the business. Then we go back and forth with your leadership team until every plan is locked in, with the blockers named up front.

  4. Mobilize

    From your plan to everyone's

    Your leadership has been part of this since Orient. Now it goes to the whole company: a roadshow that brings the promise and the plan to the people who'll carry them out.

  5. Connect

    The promise, down to every touchpoint

    Your teams keep doing their work. Together we map each priority customer's journey, touchpoint by touchpoint, with the people who own them. Each team sees what the promise asks of them, and the gaps become the roadmap.

  6. Evolve

    Keep it holding

    Runs alongside Connect. The first time around, it sets who's accountable, how progress gets measured, and which roadmap projects come first. After each cycle we look back: did we get where we set out to go, and is the company becoming one that keeps its promise? Each year, the cycle returns to Chart.

How the work is packaged

Questions we get about customer centricity

What is customer centricity?
Making decisions around the customers who drive your business, not around channels, functions or last year's plan. We agree with it. We just don't think it's enough on its own.
Why does customer centricity fail on its own?
Because a business is a relationship with three sides. Chase the customer without a brand that means something, and you end up trying to please everyone. Chase the customer with an organization whose targets reward something else, and nothing changes on the shop floor. The brand, the customers and the organization have to move together, around one promise.
How is it different from customer experience?
Customer experience is what the customer lives at each touchpoint. Customer centricity is how the company decides what to do. Both depend on knowing which customers come first and what you've promised them, which is where we start.
How do you measure it?
Not with a single score. We look at whether your priority customers are growing in value, whether the brand means the same thing inside the company and outside it, and whether targets, budgets and incentives reward keeping the promise.
