We call them imaginary customers.

The customer a company builds for but never quite wins. It comes in 3 forms.

  1. Who you pick

    The wrong customer

    Made up, too rare to build on, or real but not a fit for your brand or the business you want to build. A muse can inspire a brand. It can't be the only customer you sell to.

  2. How you read them

    The misunderstood customer

    The right customer, read wrong, so what you build never lands with them.

  3. How you execute

    The Frankenstein customer

    Understood at the top, then stitched together from channel, region and team targets until nobody is talking to the real person. So they drift away.

Whatever the form, the result is the same. The right customers barely show up in your sales.

Inside the company, it plays out in 2 ways.

Aligned around the wrong customer. Everyone points the same way, just not at the right person.

Divided among many. Everyone names the same customer, but each paints a different picture.

The second is worse. It's better to be aligned around the wrong customer than divided among many.

What it feels like

  • Every team is busy, but results aren't moving.
  • The same decisions keep coming back to the table.
  • No two teams seem to be on the same page.
  • Holding last year's number takes more budget every year.

Growth that stays out of reach, no matter how much effort and money go in.

Customer centricity is half the story.

Focus only on the customer and it fails, like any relationship where one side does all the work. A brand people buy because they want it, not because they need it, has to hold 3 things in balance: the brand, the customers and the organization, tied together by one promise.

When they don't line up, the cracks show in 3 places, often all at once.

Empty vessels
The brand stops at a product, a symbol or a set of values, so each team fills the gaps on its own.
Imaginary customers
The business serves a customer who isn't there, doesn't drive value, or gets pieced together from channel targets until no one recognizes them.
The emperor's new clothes
The organization believes it keeps the promise, but its targets, budgets and incentives work against it, and no one says so.
